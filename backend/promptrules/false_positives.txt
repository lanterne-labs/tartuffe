It is NOT a contradiction when ANY of these false-positive patterns apply — these OVERRIDE a YES. Each is tagged with its rule token in brackets.

- [third_party_narrative] Applies ONLY when NEITHER statement gives the person's own words, position, attitude, or action AND neither is an authoritative finding (conviction, official conclusion) — e.g. two outlets describing an event the person never personally commented on. A statement of what the person said, declared, felt, promised, or did ("X said Y", "X s'est dit motivé", "X a déclaré Z") COUNTS as the person's own utterance — that is NOT this bucket, so do not drop it here. ONE ACT, TWO DESCRIPTIONS: when one statement is an outlet's LABEL of an act ("called for the president's resignation", "a appelé à la démission") and the other is the person's own wording of that SAME act refusing the label ("I am not calling for his resignation, I ask for an early election"), it is one position described twice: NO. A reversal needs two acts or two statements of the person's own. A PRIVATE remark the press REPORTS ("selon l'Opinion", "aurait confié", "a menacé en privé, selon un proche") set against the person's own PUBLIC words on the same point is the press's account against the person's: a doubtful fact, not a contradiction, NO. Prod 2349 (2026-11-02).
- [different_subjects] Different subjects despite overlapping keywords (the person's own belief vs an external party's action; what Parliament did vs what the person told colleagues). That something happened and that the person could not do it THEMSELVES are compatible: another actor did it ("the messages were extracted on August 16" vs "I could not ask for an extraction, I have no account"). A word used with DIFFERENT meanings across the two facts (a homonym, e.g. "primaire" = école primaire vs une primaire électorale) is NOT a shared subject. So is WHOSE commitment: "Trump keeps his commitments" and "Trump now sees that Russia does not keep its commitments" are about two different parties. So is WHO DID an act both statements name: "no ambush was set against him" (denying an ambush by the person's side) and "the other group set a trap and provoked the fight" assign the ambush to opposite actors and tell one story, not a reversal. So is WHOSE reform or plan: a possessive names the person's OWN proposal ("le maintien de SA réforme des retraites", "son projet", "notre réforme"), not the law in force on that topic; keeping one's own reform (e.g. the return to 62) agrees with demanding the repeal of the government's reform. EXCEPTION: denouncing or blaming a SPECIFIC NAMED person or party and then allying, merging lists, or partnering with that SAME target is a real reversal — answer YES. An ALLIANCE or a PRIMARY for one election or one perimeter and one for another are different questions: refusing a primary "de toute la gauche" (with LFI) and joining the closed primary of the PS and Place publique; excluding an accord with a party for the presidential and leaving a door open to cooperation in the legislative or local elections. A reversal needs the same perimeter, or a statement that covers both. A JUDGMENT of a leader ("Mélenchon est devenu le boulet de la gauche") is not a refusal to work with that leader's party or deputies: a later call to bring the left together including that party is a reversal only if the person had refused that cooperation itself. After a mere JUDGMENT, the DENOUNCE-THEN-ALLY exception needs a FORMAL alliance with the same target (a merged list, a coalition, a government), not a call for rapprochement; after a PLEDGE ("there will never be an alliance with X"), any call to unite with X or joint rally with X breaks it. A vote for a TARGET or an objective (renovation goals "backed by the energy-saving certificates") is not a position on the TOOL named as its means: a later pledge to abolish that tool contradicts the vote only if the ballot's own object was the tool.
- [different_events] Genuinely unrelated actions, bills, cases, or occasions. A YEARLY text named without its year (the budget, the PLF, the PLFSS, the loi de finances) at dates about a year apart is TWO texts: "we will vote against this budget" in October 2025 (the 2026 budget) and "I plan to amend the budget rather than block it" in October 2026 (the 2027 budget) are positions on two different texts, not a reversal, unless a statement ties them to the same one. NOT this bucket: a stated commitment and a later action that walks it back (e.g. "promised to keep retirement at 62" vs "abandoned the repeal of the move to 64") — that is one broken commitment, answer YES. The same holds for ELECTIONS years apart (hesitating to run in 2022 and declaring for 2027), for an AMENDMENT, an ARTICLE or an EXEMPTION named without an identifier in two successive finance texts, for two MEETINGS with different conveners, for two GOVERNMENTS: different objects unless a statement ties them. A statement that bounds ONE text in preparation ("this bill will not go beyond what was already voted", said as minister in 2024) binds that text only: a programme proposed years later, in another role (as a candidate), is another text, not a broken commitment. An ARTICLE of a bill voted at two READINGS (première lecture, deuxième lecture, CMP) is two versions when its wording changed between them: a CONTRE then a POUR on "l'article 17" is a position on each version, not a reversal, unless the two descriptions of the article are the same. An ASSESSMENT of a situation at its date ("les finances publiques sont tenues", 2024) is not a position a plan years later ("un frein d'urgence de 20 milliards", 2026) can reverse: the situation changed; a reversal needs a position on what should be done. So is a whole BILL rewritten between the person's opposition and their vote: opposing the government's bill, then voting the text rewritten in CMP or by the Senate while saying it changed or why they now vote it, is a position on each version, NO; YES only when the text voted is the one opposed. Prod 2392 (2026-11-04).
- [numbers_measurements] Different counts, percentages, vote-shares, or poll figures of the same quantity at different times, sources, or electoral rounds. Different AGGREGATES are different quantities, not one figure: the State budget deficit ("le déficit budgétaire de l'État") and the public deficit ("le déficit public", every administration with the social security and local authorities), a total in euros and a share of GDP, a gross and a net figure. "The State deficit fell by 17 billion" and "the deficit rose last year" can both be true. Several outlets' figures of ONE presentation (the same plan, the same day) are one set of figures read differently: a primary balance in 18 months, a 3 percent total deficit in 2030 and 2.2 percent in 2032 are three measures of one plan, not a changed promise; two outlets contradict each other, not the person.
- [forecast_vs_current] One is a forecast or projection about the future, the other a current or regional figure. Two conditional forecasts whose IF-clauses differ ("if the government falls on the budget, there will be no budget" and "even censured, we will at least have a budget") describe two scenarios, not two positions.
- [accusation_not_finding] An accusation, indictment, summons, or ongoing trial paired with a denial. Procedural steps are not guilt; only a conviction or official conclusion breaks a denial. A prosecutor's réquisition (requested sentence) is likewise only a request by a third party — it is neither a finding nor an act of self-exemption by the person. This bucket never covers the person's own claim of innocence AFTER a conviction: an appeal or a cassation appeal lodged "to establish her innocence" is such a claim, and it contradicts the conviction (keep).
- [legal_process_stages] Accusation, indictment, trial, and conviction are sequential stages of the SAME process; being accused then convicted is normal progression.
- [conviction_vs_acknowledgment] A conviction does not contradict admitting imperfections, error, or negligence; good faith is compatible with negligence. Only an explicit denial of the SAME wrongdoing a conviction then establishes is a contradiction. A claim to have been convicted "only for opinions" / "pour des délits d'opinion" AGREES with a conviction for WORDS (provocation à la haine, injure raciste, diffamation, incitation à la discrimination): it characterises such convictions, it does not deny them. Only a conviction for an ACT that is not speech (contrefaçon, fraude, violence, détournement) contradicts it.
- [complementary_facts] Different ASPECTS of the same ruling or event (prison term plus ineligibility; first-instance plus appeal sentence) are supplementary, not contradictory. Two compatible FACETS of one position expressed together are likewise not a reversal — e.g. a moral judgment ("he should have resigned") alongside a practical assessment ("but his resignation would serve no purpose") is one nuanced stance. So are two CAUSES of one failure: naming the PRIMARY culprit or ruling out ONE cause ("le premier responsable est le meurtrier, et non un manque de moyens") and later admitting OTHER failures (errors, negligence in the chain) — "premier" is not "seul", and errors are not a lack of means. Two statements of the SAME day in the SAME interview, hearing or speech that qualify each other ("what goes to referendum is the ban on islamism and its expressions, not the veil as such" and "a referendum on banning the veil in all public buildings"; "I did not demand 72-hour custody by a stroke of the pen" and the instruction he sent to prosecutors) are facets of one position, unless the person explicitly corrects themself.
- [coherent_defense] Multiple denials about DIFFERENT aspects of one case form a single consistent defense. Only admitting a claim then denying that SAME claim is a contradiction.
- [procedural_scope] Different scopes or components of the SAME action (a 49-3 on the revenue part vs the expenditure part vs the overall budget). Procedure on different parts of one measure is not inconsistency. A targeted or PARTIAL measure for specific groups does not contradict declining the TOTAL or universal version of that same measure — "total"/"universelle"/"généralisée" marks a different scope (e.g. free transport for seniors and hospital staff vs ruling out fully free transport for everyone; doubling a tax-free fuel PREMIUM, or extending aid to the most exposed sectors, vs refusing a general fuel-tax cut or generalized fuel aid).
- [condition_later_met] One fact is conditional ("I would do X IF Y happens") and the other shows Y came true or X happening — that is consistent, not a contradiction. The SAME applies when the condition did NOT occur: if a DIFFERENT outcome happened (e.g. candidacy promised IF a SUSPENDED sentence, but the person received a FIRM sentence), the conditional was never triggered and the commitment is untouched. A later claim that NO such scenario is LEFT ("there is no longer any scenario in which I could not run", "the risk is behind me") says the antecedent is ruled out: it agrees with an earlier plan for that antecedent ("IF the ban is confirmed, someone else will run"), since the plan was for a case that did not come. A conditional is broken ONLY if its exact antecedent occurred and the person then did the opposite of what they pledged. "If my sentence is confirmed on appeal" (« si ma peine est confirmée en appel ») OCCURRED when the appeal upheld the conviction, even with a lighter or partly suspended sentence or without provisional execution: running afterwards breaks it (keep). Only an acquittal, or an antecedent naming ONE specific penalty the appeal did not apply (the ban on office with immediate effect), leaves it unmet. When it did occur and the person announces, keeps or pursues the very thing they said they would not do under it (running, campaigning, staying in office), the conditional IS broken: that is a STATED-CONDITION WALK-BACK, answer YES. Do not ask for proof that the sanction is being carried out at that moment, nor that it overlaps the campaign: the announcement under the condition breaks the pledge. Whether the pledged action has happened YET is irrelevant and is never a reason to hedge: a court ruling or event that merely fulfils the IF-condition cannot contradict the pledge, so the answer is a firm NO / remove, not "ambiguous". A carve-out breaks a commitment only when it excludes the very activity committed to: a condition on the person's OWN CANDIDACY ("I will not be a candidate under a bracelet", "my candidacy depends on the verdict") and a commitment to CAMPAIGN with a named running mate whatever the verdict are compatible, since campaigning for the ticket does not require being its candidate; a refusal to campaign AT ALL under the condition would break it. A pledge to a SINGLE CANDIDACY with a named rival of the same camp ("X and Y will not run against each other", one of them steps aside before the election) is a withdrawal mechanism, not a renunciation: being a declared candidate, campaigning, seeking a nomination or saying "at this stage neither steps aside" are all compatible with it. It is broken only when the person keeps running after the other was designated. Seeking allies, a coalition or a deal to REACH the condition the person set ("I will not go to Matignon without an absolute majority", then "if a few deputies are missing, I will reach out to others") pursues that condition: only taking the office WITHOUT it breaks the pledge. PRIMARY PLEDGES: a pledge to rally behind, or not to hinder, the winner of a primary binds only once a primary is held and someone else wins it. Declaring one's candidacy « primaire ou pas », proposing a charter that binds the losers to rally, and refusing to rally to a rival OUTSIDE any primary are all compatible with it. Not hindering is not endorsing: a promise not to get in the winner's way is broken by running against or obstructing them, not by withholding support. A call for a COLLECTIVE act (« on doit tous quitter X ») is likewise conditional on the others: the person not acting alone once the collective act did not happen, and saying so (staying « faute d'un départ collectif »), is NO. Prod 2409, 2413, 2405 (2026-11-04).
- [consistent_ignorance] Claims of not knowing about DIFFERENT aspects of one situation reinforce each other. Only demonstrated knowledge that another statement denies is a contradiction.
- [subjective_vs_objective] "I wasn't aware of X" (subjective) and "X didn't happen" (objective) are compatible; denying X does not imply prior knowledge of X.
- [paraphrase_same_position] Both statements take the SAME side or support the SAME claim, regardless of wording, language, or level of detail. Map each statement to the position it IMPLIES before comparing: opposing or criticizing a measure is the SAME position as supporting its opposite (e.g. "the statute of limitations protects perpetrators" means wanting non-prescription, which equals defending imprescriptibility). Do not read a double negative as a reversal. Resolve vocabulary before comparing: "imprescriptibilité"/"non-prescription" means NO statute of limitations, so defending imprescriptibilité and calling prescription protective of perpetrators are the SAME stance.
- [same_direction] Both facts point the SAME way. Includes follow-through (an action that FULFILLS, ADVANCES, or IMPLEMENTS a stated goal — if the reason says B "fulfills", "implements", or "is the concrete action of" A, answer NO) and criticism of non-enforcement (calling a rule "useless unless enforced" or "not applied" wants it applied, not opposed). Budget French: "un effort (supplémentaire) sur les dépenses" means spending CUTS, the same direction as a spending freeze, not more spending. "No other solution than X" / "the only way is X" followed by a second measure Y toward the same goal (cut fuel taxes, and also lift the constraints on fuel distributors) adds a lever: it is not dropping X, so not a reversal; it would be one only if X were abandoned or opposed. Asking again for a provision that Parliament ADOPTED and a court CENSURED (the Conseil constitutionnel on procedure) is within "what was already voted", not beyond it. Two facts that blame the SAME cause (lower receipts, not higher spending) state ONE position; a reason that reads a second cause into a fact that does not name it is unsupported: answer NO. Prod 2336 (2026-11-01).
- [announcement_then_action] Announcing, promising, or planning something then DOING it is a fulfilled commitment. Contradictory only if the person did the OPPOSITE of what they announced. A decision DEFERRED to after an event ("I will wait for the general policy speech before deciding") and the decision taken once that event has passed ("I censure everything", three days later, after the speech) is the announced sequence, whatever the decision; deferring is not a position on the decision itself. A pledge to carry out a measure ("nous assumerons d'avancer sur la réforme") is not broken when its implementation is SUSPENDED or POSTPONED under an event outside the person's choice (a snap election called by someone else, a censure, a court ruling, the dissolution of the Assembly): only the person abandoning, withdrawing or opposing the measure breaks it. Prod 2354 (2026-11-02). A pledge NARROWED (a smaller version of the same measure) or POSTPONED (a later date for it) by the person BEFORE any act, keeping its direction (« abroger dès l'automne », then the immediate repeal set aside for a costed later one), is a revised programme, NO. It stays YES when the person renounces the measure or states its opposite (proposing to scrap two public holidays, then « je ne supprimerai aucun jour férié »), when a later ACT goes against the pledge, or when the person denies having made it. Prod 2414 (2026-11-04).
- [vague_vs_specific] One statement vague or general, the other specific, with no clear sign they refer to the same thing. If context (names, dates, events) shows they ARE the same thing, it IS a contradiction. A QUALIFIED general position (opposing X "en permanence" / systematically / "as a rule") is compatible with endorsing ONE specific instance of X — the qualifier scopes the stance to repeated/systematic cases, so a single specific case does not break it. EXCEPTION: a normative RULE or SANCTION the person publicly demanded for a CLASS of people ("anyone convicted should be ineligible / must resign / should be barred") is NOT vague once the person themselves demonstrably enters that class — it binds them too, and their own act exempting themselves from that same sanction IS a contradiction (self-exempting double standard) — answer YES. This EXCEPTION requires BOTH sides to be the person's OWN words or acts: one side states the rule, the OTHER side must be them claiming or taking the exemption ("je suis éligible", "rien ne m'empêche d'être candidate", "j'annonce ma candidature"). If the other side is instead something DONE TO them by the justice system or by anyone else — a sentence, a court or appeal ruling, a prosecutor's request, an awaiting-verdict account — answer NO: undergoing the sanction is what their own rule prescribes, so it AGREES with the rule instead of breaking it. A DENIAL hedged by a qualifier (« je n'ai jamais dit non PAR PRINCIPE à la primaire ») is not contradicted by a practical objection (« une usine à gaz ») to ONE FORM of the thing (a primary widened to the centre): the denial is about principle, the objection about form. Prod 2329 (2026-11-01). A class rule binds the OFFICE it names and the person THEMSELF: a rule for parliamentarians (« pas de condamnation au casier pour être parlementaire ») is not broken by welcoming a convicted ALLY's presidential candidacy; the exception needs the person in the class, claiming the exemption for their own case. Prod 2379 (2026-11-04).
- [geographic_scope] A regional, departmental, or local fact does not contradict national-level data.
- [learning_over_time] Earlier "didn't know" then later "knew" is consistent (learned over time). EVOLUTION OF OPINION (never for an opinion the person changes once a rule or sanction applies to THEMSELF, which is a self-exempting double standard at any gap): two opposite OPINIONS of the person on one question (no act, no promise, no denial) more than about THREE MONTHS apart are an evolution, not a contradiction: everybody may change their mind (hostile to Corsican autonomy years ago, for it now). So is a change the person acknowledges or explains, at any gap ("I changed my mind because of the nationalists' results since 2015"). This bucket never covers an ACT against stated words (a vote, a decision, an alliance, a candidacy), a broken PROMISE or pledge ("never an alliance with X", then the alliance), a stated-condition walk-back, a DENIAL of one's own earlier position, or a self-exempting double standard: those stay YES at any gap. Earlier "claimed X" then later "denied X" IS a contradiction. ORDER: a past-tense denial ("I have never proposed X", "je ne me suis jamais situé vis-à-vis de Y") is a claim about the past up to its own date: only an act or statement of X dated BEFORE it can contradict it; one dated after is a later line, NO. Conversely a pledge about FUTURE conduct ("I will never criticise Z") is broken only by an act dated AFTER it; an earlier act cannot break it. Claiming knowledge then disclaiming it stays a contradiction: asserting a thesis on a subject, then LATER saying "it is not my subject, I have not studied it" is claim then denial, YES. A disclaimer dated BEFORE the thesis it is paired with is not a denial of that thesis: NO for that pair. Two opposite OPINIONS with NO DATE on one side or both cannot show the three-month window: they are an evolution too, remove (not ambiguous); only a dated pair inside the window stays. Prod 152 (2026-11-01).
- [date_only_no_commitment] Only the date or timing of an event differs, with no personal commitment about that timing being broken. Default NO. Likewise an undated intention ("je réviserai la Constitution") and "not now / not in the current period" are compatible: a timing qualifier is not a reversal. A promise WITH a date or a deadline, then broken, stays a contradiction. A denial of a CURRENT thought or plan with no pledge about the future ("je n'y pense pas", "ce n'est pas d'actualité", "I am not thinking about 2027") commits to nothing: a decision taken a year or more later (a candidacy) is a change of mind, NO. Declining to argue about X NOW (« je ne vais pas perdre du temps à ergoter sur une primaire », « je n'ai jamais rien exclu ») or saying others block it is not a position against X: later supporting X is NO. Prod 2400 (2026-11-04).
- [endorsement_vs_non_candidacy] Endorsing or backing ANOTHER named person for an office while stating you are not a candidate yourself: backer and candidate are compatible roles. Does NOT apply when the person themselves heads, leads, or conducts a list ("liste conduite par X", "tête de liste"), is elected, or wins seats — leading a list IS being a candidate, so "not a candidate" followed by leading a list is a real reversal (YES).
- [relative_vote_preference] AMENDMENT BALLOTS ONLY. A vote on an amendement/sous-amendement judges that modification, in context: voting it down implies neither support for the unamended text, nor opposition to the parent bill (rejecting a rival group's dilatory amendment to a bill one champions IS championing it), nor endorsement of the modification's opposite. Drop claims that invert an amendment vote's meaning. Never use this bucket for votes on the text itself (l'ensemble, article, motion), nor when the ballot was cast AGAINST the very measure the person explicitly demanded — that one breaks their own principle: keep.
- [symbolic_counter_gesture] A small, symbolic, or immaterial action in the other direction does not undermine a stated value; the action must MATERIALLY undermine the value on the same axis. A pledge of LOYALTY or unity (to a leader, a party, a government) is not broken by a disagreement, an argument or an ultimatum INSIDE that camp ("c'est elle ou moi" about a place on a list): it is broken by leaving, running against, voting down or publicly disowning the one it was pledged to.
